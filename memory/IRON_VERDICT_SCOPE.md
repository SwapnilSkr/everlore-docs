# The Iron Verdict — final scope

Supersedes the 50/40 numbers in `IRON_VERDICT_EXPANSION.md`. The three-realm
frame, the archetype-sprite idea, the enterable/map-presence split and the
anchor-motif rule all survive unchanged — only the counts move.

**The 15 Tier A assets in flight remain valid.** Third revision in a row where
that is true, because the plate seams and the style bible were designed to be
scope-independent.

---

## 1. Why 30 is richer than 50

Three reasons, in order of weight.

**The binding constraint is writing, not art.** Fifty places was never fifty
paintings — it was fifty reasons to exist, fifty reasons to return, and fifty
sets of people. Art scales with money. Authored content scales with attention,
and attention is the thing we actually have least of.

**Richness is connection density, not count.** The number that predicts whether
a world feels alive is not how many places it has. It is *how many places each
character moves through* and *how many other characters each one knows*. A
character who appears in four places and has opinions about six people
generates more perceived world than four characters who each sit in one room.
Cutting breadth is what buys the budget to make characters mobile.

**The freed budget buys state, and state is what feels alive.** Thirty places
costs ~72 assets against a 100 budget. The 28 spare go into *variants* —
the same place at night, the forge lit, the camp after the pact, a character's
second expression. Count makes a world feel large. State makes it feel alive.
On a phone screen, alive beats large every time, and it is not close.

The failure mode at 50 was a hundred thin places, which the production plan
already warns about. The failure mode at 30 is a slightly smaller map — which
is barely a failure at all.

---

## 2. The shape

### 30 places = 20 enterable + 10 map-presence

Cut from the enterable side, not the map-presence side. A map-presence place
costs one shared archetype sprite and one sentence of fact; it is the cheapest
open-world feeling we can buy and we should not economise on it.

| Realm | Places | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Aldermere** | 16 | Chapters I and III. Must be dense — the succession happens here |
| **Vaskir** | 7 | Chapter II. Seven excellent places, no filler |
| **Serevane** | 7 | Chapter III. Same |

Seven concentrated places in a realm you visit for one chapter beats fourteen
half-used ones. A foreign country should feel *specific*, not comprehensive.

### 5 plates, not 7

```
Vaskir      1 plate    snow, pine, fjord
── seam: snow-capped mountain ridge ──
Aldermere   3 plates   the duchy (already ordered)
── seam: open sea ──
Serevane    1 plate    islands, docks, canals
                       total ~2048 x 10240
```

This is the counter-intuitive one: **fewer plates makes the map feel bigger.**
Thirty places over seven plates is four per plate and reads as empty. Thirty
over five is six per plate and reads as a populated country. Emptiness reads as
unfinished, not as scale.

### 30 characters, in three tiers

| Tier | Count | Gets | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Principal** | 12 | bespoke portrait + expression variant | Drives the spine. Moves between 3+ places. Has bond meters, a want, a secret, and a reason to refuse |
| **Supporting** | 10 | bespoke portrait | One strong function, one secret, one location |
| **Framed** | 8 | none — earns a codex card from recurrence | Heard, quoted, met at distance |

20 bespoke portraits, 18 of them new (Cassian and Nara exist). Five
romanceable, spread across all three realms.

That surplus allocation is the actual recommendation. Everything above it is
arithmetic; this is the judgement call.

## 4. The one real risk, and it is not art

Thirty named characters across thirty places is a genuine stress test for
presence tracking and recall scoping — which the system risk map already lists
as live weaknesses. The failure looks like a Vaskir thane being retrieved into
a Serevane scene, or someone seated in a room they left two chapters ago.

Two mitigations, both cheap and both worth doing before the cast grows:

1. **Scope casts regionally.** A character from one realm should be nearly
   unretrievable while the player is in another, absent an explicit reason.
   That bounds the recall problem by construction instead of by re-ranking.
2. **Move characters deliberately, not incidentally.** A principal appearing in
   three places should have three *authored* reasons to be there. Mobility is
   what makes them feel alive; unexplained mobility is what makes presence
   tracking wrong.

The art risk at this scale is now modest. The consistency risk is the one to
watch, and it is a systems problem we already know how to name.
